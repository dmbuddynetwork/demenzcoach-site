“It's getting dark, I have to go!” – when someone with dementia is unsettled in the evening

What's behind it

Many people with dementia feel more unsettled in the late afternoon and evening.

What you can say

“It's getting dark. Let's turn the lights on.”

“You're safe here with me.”

“Let's have a cup of tea together.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Turn on lights early and close the curtains.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I have to leave before it gets dark.”, “When it gets dark, I have to go.”

If that doesn't help

“Come on, let's close the curtains and put on some music.”

Change the scene: lights on, curtains closed, a familiar song, or a short walk together around the house.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Fading light, tiredness and old routines can all come together. You can make the evening bright, quiet and familiar.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Fading light and shadows.
  • Tiredness after the day.
  • An old routine, like going home after work.
  • Feeling unsure as the day changes.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • arguing about where they need to go
  • lots of noise and visitors in the evening
  • holding them back without explanation

What can help next

  • Plan a quiet, familiar activity for the late afternoon.
  • Keep visits and errands for the morning.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
