“Where are my children?” – when someone with dementia worries about small children

What's behind it

Often they are in a time when the children were small and needed them.

What you can say

“The children are fine. They're being looked after.”

“You've always taken such good care of them.”

“Tell me about your children when they were little.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Look at photos from back then together.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “The little ones need to go to bed.”, “I have to cook for the kids.”

If that doesn't help

“Everyone is safe. Could you help me fold these?”

Respond to the caring: give them something to look after, like laundry to fold or a soft blanket, and stay close.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Behind it is care and responsibility. You can respond to that and let them know the children are fine.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Memories of a time when the children were little.
  • Feeling responsible and wanting to look after someone.
  • Worry and unease.
  • Wanting to be needed.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • insisting “Your children are 50 now”
  • saying the children aren't coming
  • long explanations

What can help next

  • Give a small caring task, like folding clothes or setting the table.
  • If they often worry in the evening, plan something quiet for then.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
