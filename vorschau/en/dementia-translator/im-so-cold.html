“I'm so cold” – when someone with dementia keeps saying they're cold

What's behind it

Feeling cold is uncomfortable and can make someone feel unsettled too.

What you can say

“You're cold. I'll get you a blanket.”

“Let's put on a warm sweater.”

“Would you like a warm drink?”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Check clothes, socks, blanket and the room temperature.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I'm freezing.”, “It's freezing in here.”

If that doesn't help

“Here's your blanket. I'll make us something warm.”

Make it warmer instead of discussing it: a blanket, socks, a warm drink, and sit close.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Take it seriously, even if the room seems warm to you.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • They really do feel cold.
  • Thin clothes or sitting still for a long time.
  • Feeling uneasy or lonely.
  • A wish to be looked after.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • saying “It's warm in here”
  • brushing it off
  • arguing about the temperature

What can help next

  • A warm drink and a little movement.
  • Let them choose their own clothes when possible.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
