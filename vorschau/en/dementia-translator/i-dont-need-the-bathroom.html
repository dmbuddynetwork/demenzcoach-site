“I don't need the bathroom” – going to the toilet with someone with dementia

What's behind it

Going to the toilet is very private. Shame, not feeling the need or not finding the way can be behind refusing.

What you can say

“I'm going to the bathroom. Come along, then we're done.”

“Let's freshen up before we go out.”

“It's all right. That can happen to anyone.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

A sign or picture on the bathroom door.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I just went.”, “I don't need the toilet.”

If that doesn't help

“Before we go, let's just stop by the bathroom.”

Try again later, casually, together with something else: before a walk or before eating.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Instead of asking directly, you can offer it casually.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Feeling embarrassed.
  • Not feeling the need.
  • Not finding the bathroom.
  • Wanting to decide for themselves.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • asking in front of others
  • scolding after an accident
  • asking again and again

What can help next

  • Regular, casual offers, like before going out.
  • Clothes that are easy to open.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
