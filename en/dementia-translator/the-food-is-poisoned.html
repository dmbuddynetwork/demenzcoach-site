“The food is poisoned” – when someone with dementia refuses food out of distrust

What's behind it

For the person, the fear feels real. A strange taste, a new face at the table or distrust can be behind it.

What you can say

“You're worried about the food. I understand.”

“Look, I'll eat some of it with you.”

“Would you like to choose what we eat?”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Eat from the same pot or plate, so they can see it.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I'm not eating anything.”, “I won't eat that.”

If that doesn't help

“Let's pick something together. You choose.”

Let them watch or help: open something sealed together, peel a banana, or let them choose.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Instead of arguing, you can make eating feel safe again.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Fear and distrust.
  • Food that tastes or looks different than usual.
  • Feeling unsure about who cooked it.
  • Simply not being hungry.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • arguing that the food is fine
  • forcing them to eat
  • getting offended

What can help next

  • Offer food in its original packaging, like a yogurt or a banana.
  • If you're worried about how much they eat or drink, talk to your doctor.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
