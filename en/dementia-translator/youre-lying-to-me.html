Your input:

“You're lying to me!”

What's behind it

When your own memory has gaps, other people can quickly seem dishonest.

What you can say

I can see you feel let down. That's hard.
I don't want to argue with you. Let's sort it out together.
I'm on your side.

What you can do

Write important things down together, so you can look them up together later.

Second try

Let's leave it. Shall we have a cup of tea?

Stop discussing who's right. Gently change the subject, offer a drink, or let another familiar person take over for a while.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

Background and more tips

Background

Defending yourself rarely gets through. You can take the feeling seriously and offer to sort it out together.

  • Gaps in memory make other people's words seem wrong.
  • Feeling left out of decisions.
  • Fear and feeling unsure.
  • Mixing up times and people.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • proving that you're right
  • saying “You've forgotten again”
  • getting angry back

What can help next

  • Include them in small decisions whenever you can.
  • Talk to someone you trust about how much this hurts.
