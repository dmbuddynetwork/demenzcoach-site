You hear:

“I'm not taking those pills” – when someone with dementia refuses tablets

What's behind it

Refusing often comes from distrust, feeling told what to do, or not knowing what the pills are for.

What you can say

“All right, we'll try again after coffee.”

“You don't have to right now. I'll put them here.”

“Let's do it together. I'll have a glass of water too.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Try again a little later, in a quieter moment.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “They just make me sick.”, “I don't need those pills.”

If that doesn't help

“All right, we'll leave it for now. Later is fine too.”

Don't argue. Put the pills away and try again later, maybe with another familiar person. Tell your doctor or pharmacist if pills are often refused.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Instead of pushing, you can try again later, in a quiet moment and without pressure.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Distrust: “What are you giving me?”
  • Wanting to decide for themselves.
  • Not knowing what the pills are for.
  • Not liking the taste or the situation.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • forcing them or arguing
  • hiding pills in food or drink on your own – talk to your doctor or pharmacist about it
  • lecturing them about their health

What can help next

  • Tell your doctor or pharmacist that pills are being refused. They can tell you what to do.
  • A fixed routine, like with breakfast, can make it more familiar.

When talking isn't enough

Questions about medicines: ask your doctor or pharmacist. This tool doesn't give advice on medicines. In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
