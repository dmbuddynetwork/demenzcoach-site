You hear:

“Who are you?” – when someone with dementia doesn't recognize you

What's behind it

Not being recognized hurts. To the person, your face may seem unfamiliar right now.

What you can say

“Hello, I'm Anna, your daughter. I'm here for you today.”

“I'm someone who cares about you very much.”

“You don't have to know right now. I'm here.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

“Anna” is just an example name – use your own. These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Say your name and how you're related at the start of each visit, without testing.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “You're not my daughter.”, “I don't know you.”

If that doesn't help

“Hello, I'm Anna. It's lovely to see you.”

Make a fresh start: step out for a moment, knock, come back in with a smile and say your name, without asking “Don't you know me?”.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Or they are looking for the daughter from an earlier time. Introduce yourself calmly, without testing or pushing.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • The picture from memory doesn't match the face of today.
  • Feeling unsure or scared because someone seems unfamiliar.
  • Dim light or a busy room make faces harder to make out.
  • Tiredness or a difficult day.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • asking “Don't you recognize me?” or quizzing them
  • showing how hurt you are in that moment
  • arguing about who you are

What can help next

  • Look at old photos together. Sometimes it's easier to talk about the past.
  • It's okay to grieve. Talk about it with people you trust.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
