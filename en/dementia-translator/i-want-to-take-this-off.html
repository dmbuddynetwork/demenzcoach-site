“I want to take this off!” – when someone with dementia undresses at the wrong time

What's behind it

Undressing often has a simple reason: too hot, uncomfortable, needing the toilet or something itching.

What you can say

“You're too warm. Let's find something lighter.”

“Come with me, we'll sort it out in the bedroom.”

“Is something uncomfortable? Show me.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Check the temperature, the fabric, labels and the toilet.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I'm so hot.”, “This is all too tight.”

If that doesn't help

“Let's go to the bedroom and find something lighter.”

Take it somewhere private: go to the bathroom or bedroom together and look for something more comfortable.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

You can look for the reason calmly, without scolding.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Feeling too hot.
  • Uncomfortable or itchy clothes.
  • Needing the toilet.
  • Thinking it's time for bed.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • scolding or shaming
  • reacting loudly
  • holding the clothes on by force

What can help next

  • If needed, clothes that open at the back or are harder to undo.
  • Guide them somewhere private before they undress.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
