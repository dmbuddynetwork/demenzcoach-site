“I'm staying in bed today” – when someone with dementia won't get up

What's behind it

Not wanting to get up can come from tiredness, low spirits or not seeing a reason to.

What you can say

“You can rest a bit longer. I'll bring you a coffee.”

“Breakfast is ready when you are.”

“It's lovely out today. Shall we look out of the window?”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Open the curtains and let daylight in.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I'm not getting up.”, “Let me lie here a bit longer.”

If that doesn't help

“Let's just sit up for a moment. Here's your coffee.”

Start smaller: sit on the edge of the bed together, have a drink, open the window.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Instead of pressure, you can make the morning inviting.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Tiredness.
  • Not seeing a reason to get up.
  • Feeling low.
  • Wanting to decide for themselves.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • pulling back the covers
  • rushing
  • listing everything that has to be done today

What can help next

  • Something to look forward to: a favorite breakfast, music.
  • If you're unsure why they stay in bed, talk to your doctor.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
