“There's someone in the room” – when someone with dementia sees things that aren't there

What's behind it

For the person, what they see is real. Arguing about it rarely gets through.

What you can say

“You can see someone there. That sounds scary. I'm with you.”

“Let's look together and turn the light on.”

“You're safe. I'm staying here.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Turn on more light, and cover mirrors or windows that reflect at night.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “There are strangers sitting there.”, “There are bugs on the wall.”

If that doesn't help

“Come with me to the kitchen. I'll make us some tea.”

Change the scene: go to another room together, turn on the light and offer a drink. Don't keep discussing what they saw.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

You can take the feeling seriously, look at the room together and make it feel safer.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Shadows, reflections or a TV can look like real people.
  • Feeling scared or unsure, especially when it's dark.
  • Memories that feel like the present.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • insisting “There's nobody there”
  • playing along with something scary as if it were true
  • laughing about it

What can help next

  • Turn off the TV or radio if voices from it could be confusing.
  • Write down when it happens, so you can describe it to your doctor.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
