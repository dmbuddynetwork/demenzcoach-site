“I have to go to work” – understanding it and answering gently

What's behind it

Often the person wants to feel needed, do something useful and carry responsibility.

What you can say

“You've always been so reliable. Tell me what's on today.”

“Before you go, could you help me with this for a moment?”

“The kids are taken care of. Let's see what needs doing now.”

Are they crying or very upset? Then just say: “I'm with you.” The sentences above help once things are a little calmer.

These are suggestions, not rules. Adjust the words and watch how they land. Add your names in the translator

What you can do

Small, real tasks like folding laundry, setting the table or watering plants give them something to do.

Didn't help? Go to the second try ↓

Said in other ways: “I have to pick up the kids.”, “I need to go, they're waiting for me.”

If that doesn't help

“I'll come with you. Let's walk a little way together first.”

Give the urge to leave a direction: coats on and a few steps outside together, or a walk to the mailbox. Coming back is often easier after that.

It's okay to stop. If it gets louder or more heated: stop talking, stop asking for anything, step back if it's safe, and start again later. If anyone is in danger: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK).

More about this

Background

Instead of contradicting them, you can honor their old role and offer a task or your company.

Every moment is different. Often it's one of these:

  • Wanting to be useful and to have a job to do.
  • An old routine that comes back at the same time of day.
  • Feeling restless and needing to move.
  • Worry about people they used to be responsible for.

Better to avoid

Things that often don't help:

  • countering with facts (“You retired 20 years ago”)
  • holding them back without explanation or correcting them in front of others
  • long explanations of why it's not possible today

What can help next

  • A short walk together gives the urge to move a direction, without arguing about where to.
  • Note the time when it comes up. Then you can plan a quiet activity just before.

When talking isn't enough

In danger, after a fall, with breathing problems or unconsciousness, or if you're unsure: call 911 (US) or 999 (UK). For medical advice, contact your doctor. This tool can't judge how urgent something is.
